In the crater where Astercote once stood, we discovered a strange black substance. Its aura was overwhelming — the unmistakable presence of an artifact.

We pressed on and soon glimpsed Valondale in the distance. Yet as we approached, the settlement fell silent. Doors were bolted, shutters slammed closed. The villagers looked upon us with fear.

There we encountered **Eutichius Colombus**, but soon I was struck by the curse of the wizard **Petrus Carulus**. Petrus revealed himself as a cruel master, sending harpies against us and warning the villagers that any plea for help from outsiders would be punished. His servants were many: a two-headed giantess who drank and slept in drunken stupor, corpses of animals that shuffled in decay, brutish furred humanoids, scratching little demons, and a shapeshifting woman whose form changed with each passing day. And still, something unseen lurked beyond the library, gurgling horribly in the dark.

In the Colomb family’s twisted legacy we met more: insectoid horrors and their masked “father,” along with their mother. **Patrick and Martina Colomb** were their true names. On our way to the library, we glimpsed two sphinxes, though they were only illusions, and passed a construct that whispered secrets of the wizard.

At last we spoke to Petrus. He revealed his ambition: he would side with whichever force proved victorious in the coming war. In exchange for retrieving a crystal needed for his telescope, he promised us firepower and pledged to remove the cursed masks from the nobles.

In pursuit of the crystal, we faced an **Allip** — a maddened, shrieking thing born of torment. Afterward, the portraits in the hallway caught our eye: among them, **Lord Alfons Colomb**, his smaller portrait marked with the star sign of the Reaper.

We met the Colomb children as well. The eldest daughter wept, fearing she would be forced to marry Petrus. The younger daughter whispered of assassination, while the son had lost his mind, claiming he was an owl.

Delving deeper, we uncovered a secret room filled with robes, coded objects, and a hidden chest. In a cold chamber below we found the body of Lord Alfons himself, preserved among deadly brown mold. Rein Sin nearly perished from its chill, but in the end, we recovered his keys and with them opened the chest. Inside lay the very telescope part Petrus had demanded.

Phoenix later discovered a hidden mechanism behind the family fireplace — a plate with the Colomb sigil and a shield that revealed a secret bookcase. Within, we also uncovered Alfons Colomb’s signet ring.

Though the eldest daughter remained terrified of Petrus, we returned the part. True to his word, Petrus rewarded us with enchanted flares. The Colomb patriarch, in turn, sought to show gratitude: he offered us a house, as well as two ships for the war effort.

That night, fever overtook me. Thankfully, Rein’s care saw me through. With the girl in our company, we departed Valondale and set our sights on Chalksmere.

We pushed ourselves hard, marching quickly, for rumors of a sickness had reached us. The land confirmed our fears: the river carried corruption, plants lay shriveled and dead along its banks. We traced the river upstream until we reached a looming building. Outside its stone gates, strange shapes shifted, attempting to hide.

They revealed themselves as **Quaggoths**, and battle was joined. Once victorious, we forced our way through the stone door and into a bizarre, hidden laboratory…
